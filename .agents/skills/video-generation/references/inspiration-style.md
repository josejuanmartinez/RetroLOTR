# RetroLOTR Video Inspiration Style

This brief synthesizes the nine images in `Assets/Art/Videos/Inspiration`. The target is a dark late-1970s hand-made fantasy-animation language: painted backgrounds, outlined cel characters, rotoscoped weight, limited-animation economy, and the tactile defects of photographed film artwork. Use the images for analysis only. Express their shared period visual grammar in text-to-video prompts.

## Core identity

The desired image should feel like a surviving frame from a dark late-1970s fantasy animation, painted and photographed by hand rather than rendered digitally. It sits between an illustrated mythic storybook, a theatrical animation cel, rotoscoped live-action movement, and a dark medieval mural. The world is earnest, mythic, weathered, and slightly uncanny. It must not feel cute, contemporary, glossy, photoreal, anime-polished, or like modern vector animation.

The most important tension is between two levels of rendering:

- Characters and important props use decisive dark contour lines, simplified planar anatomy, flat-to-minimally shaded cel colors, and strongly readable silhouettes.
- Environments use soft opaque paint, dry-brush scumbling, watercolor-like blooms, charcoal or pastel texture, atmospheric washes, and loosely suggested detail.

Preserve that separation. A fully flat background loses the painterly depth; a fully realistic character loses the vintage cel identity.

## Line, shape, and character construction

Contours are hand-drawn, dark brown to black, visibly variable in thickness, and never mechanically perfect. Lines thicken around shadowed edges and major silhouettes, then become sparse inside forms. Small construction irregularities are desirable: asymmetrical eyes, slightly wavering hems, angular fingers, uneven beard masses, and hand-shaped curves.

Figures are built from broad graphic shapes. Cloaks make triangular or bell-like silhouettes; hats, horns, swords, shoulders, and horses create unmistakable profiles. Anatomy is simplified but grounded. Heroic or supernatural figures can be elongated and angular; hobbit-like figures are rounder, shorter, and softer; threatening riders and monsters collapse into near-black masses. Faces favor large readable eyes, long noses, heavy brows, graphic hair and beard shapes, and restrained mouth animation. Expressions should read immediately from posture and silhouette before facial detail.

Costumes are medieval-fantasy garments rendered as large color fields: deep green cloaks, brown leather, muted grey armor, off-white robes, ochre tunics, simple boots, belts, scabbards, and staffs. Avoid ornamental over-design. Materials are indicated with a few folds, highlights, or scratches rather than dense texture maps.

## Paint surface and image texture

The frame should retain physical-media evidence:

- opaque gouache and watercolor background washes;
- dry-brushed foliage, rock, smoke, and cloud edges;
- charcoal, pastel, or graphite-like grain in dark landscapes;
- slight paper or cel texture and restrained analog film grain;
- tiny registration drift, soft optical edges, and modest color breathing;
- occasional dust, faint scratches, or exposure fluctuation, kept subtle enough not to become a filter gimmick.

Colors should not be perfectly uniform. Even nominally flat areas may show delicate paint density variation. Avoid plastic surfaces, ray-traced reflections, crisp procedural noise, digital bloom, modern HDR, and hyper-sharp 3D detail.

## Palette

The ensemble alternates between earthy daylight and theatrical supernatural color.

Ground the world in olive, moss, forest green, umber, raw sienna, ochre, smoke grey, slate blue, dusty teal, parchment, cream, and weathered burgundy. Keep saturation selective rather than global. Natural greens lean muted and yellowed; shadows lean charcoal, indigo, blue-grey, or deep brown rather than neutral black.

Reserve intense color for narrative accents:

- ember red or scarlet for evil eyes, molten cracks, wounds, and danger;
- sulphur yellow and orange for flame, windows, sunset, or magical light;
- cyan or turquoise breaks in storm skies and cavern haze;
- pure or near-pure white for a wizard's robe, hair, or sudden moral contrast.

The references often place one high-value or high-saturation accent against a large subdued field. Do not distribute bright color evenly across the frame.

## Lighting and atmosphere

Lighting is graphic and theatrical rather than physically simulated. Subjects may be almost silhouetted against a burning orange cloud bank, or brightly cel-lit against a dark painterly forest. Use bold value grouping: a dark mass, a mid-tone environment, and one luminous focus.

Atmosphere is essential. Smoke, dust, mist, cloud bands, cave haze, and forest gloom flatten distant detail into painted layers. Backlight can create a halo around a white robe or turn riders into ominous cutouts. Fire behaves as a simple, sharply colored animated shape inside a softly painted glow. Interiors use stained-glass amber, dusty rose, and muted red illumination instead of realistic bounce lighting.

Avoid clean studio light, neon cyberpunk color, strong lens flare, photographic bokeh, glossy rim lighting, and modern cinematic teal-orange grading. The frame should feel illuminated through pigment and exposure.

## Composition and depth

Compositions are staged like illustrated story panels or theatre tableaux. Favor a strong foreground silhouette, a clearly separated middle-ground action, and a broad painted background. Use diagonal hills, swords, staffs, trees, smoke trails, or wings to direct the eye. Groups are arranged in readable clusters with modest overlap; each figure has a distinctive silhouette.

Scale can be deliberately dramatic: a small wizard opposing an enormous winged creature; tiny travelers placed beneath monumental statues; a rider looming close against fire. Negative space—storm sky, empty forest, cavern wall—is allowed to carry mood. Perspective may be slightly flattened, but spatial relationships must remain legible.

Use camera language compatible with traditional animation: locked tableau, slow lateral pan, restrained push-in, gentle tracking move, or a controlled low-angle reveal. Avoid drone acrobatics, extreme lens distortion, rapid handheld shake, whip pans, and dense multi-shot montage unless explicitly required.

## Motion and timing

Motion should feel hand-animated and weighty, with clear key poses and readable arcs. Favor deliberate timing over constant activity. A cloak lifts in two or three broad waves; a horse pounds forward with emphatic silhouette changes; dust trails lag behind hooves; a staff rises and holds; a monster unfurls its wings as one large graphic transformation.

Combine limited-animation restraint with selective rotoscoped naturalism. Characters can hold mostly still while eyes, beard, cloak edge, smoke, or fire continue moving. When action occurs, give it believable anticipation, impact, follow-through, and mass. Background parallax should be shallow and painterly. Keep camera motion slow enough that the line art and painted fields remain readable.

Avoid fluid 60-fps smoothness, rubbery interpolation, morphing faces, swimming outlines, weightless cloth, duplicated limbs, or continuously changing costume details. Ask for stable character design and a single unbroken shot when possible.

## Mood and storytelling

The emotional register is solemn, mythic, melancholy, perilous, and sincere, with occasional warmth in fellowship scenes. Evil is represented by opacity, silhouette, red points of light, smoke, and overwhelming scale. Good or protective power is represented by pale fabric, open gesture, warm window light, or a small clear figure standing against darkness.

Do not turn the scene into parody or children's television comedy. Even rounded friendly characters belong to a dangerous old world. Expressions should be broad enough for animation but emotionally grounded.

## Audio direction

When audio is enabled, match the visual restraint. Use wind through bare branches, leather tack, distant hoofbeats, cloth movement, fire crackle, cavern resonance, muted battle noise, low choral or modal orchestral color, sparse hand percussion, and natural environmental ambience. Dialogue should be brief, grave, and clearly timed to a visible speaker. Avoid contemporary trailer booms, electronic risers, pop music, comedy effects, and constant wall-to-wall scoring.

## Canonical prompt-style block

Adapt this block to every prompt while retaining its substance:

> Dark late-1970s hand-painted fantasy feature animation, with dark variable ink contours and simplified cel-painted characters composited over richly textured gouache, watercolor, dry-brush and charcoal backgrounds. Broad readable silhouettes, restrained planar shading, earthy olive/umber/ochre/slate palette with one intense story accent, theatrical value grouping, smoky atmospheric layers and slightly flattened mythic-storybook depth. Deliberate weighty key-pose animation with selective rotoscoped naturalism, sparse secondary motion, subtle cel registration drift, paint-density variation and restrained analog film grain. Earnest mythic medieval mood; tactile, imperfect and handmade. No glossy CGI, no photorealism, no modern vector polish, no anime styling, no captions, no logos.

## Prompt assembly example

> A weathered grey pilgrim stands alone on a black ridge as nine armored riders emerge through a wall of amber smoke. In one continuous wide shot, the camera makes a very slow push toward him; he plants his staff, his cloak snaps once in the wind, and the riders' horses descend with heavy synchronized strides while dust rolls several beats behind them. The pilgrim is the only pale shape in the composition; riders remain near-black silhouettes with tiny ember-red eyes. [Canonical style block.] Audio: dry wind, distant iron tack and hoofbeats, cloth strain, a low restrained male chorus with no modern percussion.

This example demonstrates structure, not required subject matter. Replace its scene and audio details for each request.
